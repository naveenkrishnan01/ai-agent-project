# Amazon (AMZN) Stock: Current State and Outlook

## Current Trading Snapshot

Amazon stock (NASDAQ: AMZN) is trading around **$258–259** per share, down roughly 0.2% in its latest session ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMZN/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AMZN)). Key metrics include:

- **Day's range:** $255.29–$261.12
- **52-week range:** $196.00–$287.20
- **Market capitalization:** approximately $2.788 trillion
- **P/E ratio (TTM):** ~20.8, with EPS (TTM) of $12.43
- **Performance:** up ~12% year-to-date — roughly in line with the S&P 500's ~12.7% YTD gain — and up ~48.65% over five years

([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMZN/); [Fidelity](https://digital.fidelity.com/prgw/digital/research/quote/dashboard/summary?symbol=AMZN))

Amazon is classified under Consumer Cyclical / Internet Retail, is headquartered in Seattle, Washington, employs roughly 1,595,000 full-time workers, and operates on a fiscal year ending December 31 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMZN/)).

## Q3 2025 Earnings: A Turning Point

Amazon's third-quarter 2025 results, released after market close on October 30, 2025, marked a turning point for the stock. The company beat expectations on both the top and bottom lines:

- **EPS:** $1.95 vs. $1.58 expected
- **Revenue:** $180.2 billion vs. $177.8 billion expected

([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/amazon-stock-soars-to-record-high-as-aws-growth-powers-q3-results-155934016.html))

The standout was **Amazon Web Services (AWS)**, whose revenue rose **20% year-over-year to $33.006 billion** — its fastest growth pace since 2022 and well ahead of estimates for a 17.95% increase. AWS now generates **about 60% of Amazon's total operating income**, despite accounting for a much smaller share of overall revenue ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/30/aws-q3-2025-earnings-report-amazon-cloud.html); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/amazon-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-largely-below-estimates-2025-10-30/); [Amazon IR](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2025/Amazon-com-Announces-Third-Quarter-Results/)).

CEO Andy Jassy highlighted the momentum: *"AWS is growing at a pace we haven't seen since 2022... We continue to see strong demand in AI and core infrastructure, and we've been focused on accelerating capacity"* ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/amazon-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-largely-below-estimates-2025-10-30/)).

The market reaction was dramatic: shares jumped roughly **14% in after-hours trading**, adding about **$330 billion** in market value. Had that gain held through regular trading, it would have been Amazon's biggest one-day percentage gain since 2015. The stock touched a **record high** following the report ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/amazon-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-largely-below-estimates-2025-10-30/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/amazon-stock-soars-to-record-high-as-aws-growth-powers-q3-results-155934016.html)).

The rebound was especially notable given that Amazon had been the **worst-performing "Magnificent Seven" stock** in 2025 up to that point, weighed down partly by concerns that it lagged peers in AI development. As one Zacks strategist put it, the earnings report showed that "the company's fundamentals never meaningfully weakened" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/amazon-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-largely-below-estimates-2025-10-30/)).

## Guidance and Capital Spending

Amazon guided for **Q4 2025 net sales of $206–$213 billion**, slightly above the average analyst estimate of about $208.12 billion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/amazon-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-largely-below-estimates-2025-10-30/)).

On spending, CFO Brian Olsavsky said full-year 2025 capital expenditures would reach **approximately $125 billion**, driven largely by AI infrastructure investment, with capex expected to climb **even higher in 2026**. Through the first three quarters of 2025, Amazon had already spent **$89.9 billion** in capex ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/amazon-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-largely-below-estimates-2025-10-30/)).

## Risk Factors to Watch

Despite the bullish momentum, several risks remain in play:

- **Regulatory scrutiny:** Amazon faces DOJ scrutiny over rising beef prices and an ongoing FTC lawsuit, both cited by analysts as factors to monitor ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMZN/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/markets/stocks/AMZN)).
- **Labor tensions:** Reports of strikes affecting logistics operations, including a reported "Riverside strike," point to ongoing workforce friction.
- **Elevated capital expenditures:** While framed as necessary AI infrastructure investment, spending at this scale ($125B+ in 2025, rising further in 2026) could pressure margins if growth doesn't keep pace.

## Business Segments

Amazon's operations span three primary reporting segments — **North America, International, and AWS** — supplemented by advertising, Prime subscriptions, devices (Kindle, Fire TV, Echo, Ring, Blink, eero), and media content ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMZN/)).

## Summary

Amazon's stock has staged a notable comeback after lagging its "Magnificent Seven" peers for much of 2025. The catalyst was a stronger-than-expected Q3 2025 earnings report, highlighted by AWS's reacceleration to 20% revenue growth amid robust AI-driven demand. The beat-and-raise quarter propelled shares to a record high in a historic single-day surge. Looking ahead, aggressive capital expenditure plans — roughly $125 billion in 2025, with further increases planned for 2026 — underscore Amazon's continued bet on AI infrastructure. Wall Street's consensus price targets (roughly $323–$333) suggest most analysts still see meaningful upside from current levels near $255–259, though regulatory scrutiny (DOJ, FTC) and labor issues remain worth watching.
