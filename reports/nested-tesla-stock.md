# Tesla Stock (TSLA): Current State, Financial Performance, and Outlook

## Current Price & Trading Data

Tesla shares have recently traded in the **$351–$364** range, including at least one session with a sharp decline of **5.9%–6.5%** to around $352. Over the past year, the stock has swung dramatically—from a 52-week low of **$297.38** (July 29) to a high of **$498.83** (December 22).

In November 2025 alone, TSLA moved between $391 and $442 across different sessions, underscoring the stock's significant week-to-week volatility. This is consistent with Tesla's **beta of 1.91**, meaning the stock is nearly twice as volatile as the broader market.

Tesla's market capitalization stands at approximately **$1.39–1.41 trillion**. The stock's trailing P/E ratio has ranged from roughly 324x to 352x depending on timing, but on a full-year 2025 diluted-EPS basis, the year-end P/E hit **418.19**—more than double the 198.13 level from 2024. This reflects a valuation built almost entirely on expectations of future growth rather than current profitability. Reinforcing this point, Tesla's five-year expected PEG ratio has ranged from **4.26 to 8.98** in recent quarters, indicating the stock trades at a steep premium even relative to its own projected growth.

## Q3 2025 Results: Record Revenue, Falling Profits

Tesla's third-quarter 2025 results (reported October 2025) told a story of record top-line growth paired with deteriorating profitability:

- **Revenue**: $28.095 billion, up 12% year-over-year—a quarterly record that beat Wall Street's $26.4 billion estimate.
- **Deliveries**: A record 497,099 vehicles (+7% YoY), driven largely by 481,166 Model 3/Y units.
- **Net income**: GAAP net income of $1.4 billion; non-GAAP net income of $1.8 billion, down 29% YoY on a comparable basis.
- **Operating income**: Fell approximately 40% YoY despite record revenue, as automotive gross margin came in around 18% (GAAP), or 15.4% excluding regulatory credits.
- **Free cash flow**: A record $3.99 billion, up 46% YoY.
- **Energy segment**: Revenue of $3.415 billion (+44% YoY), with energy storage deployment up 81% YoY to 12.5 GWh—the company's fastest-growing business line.

## Q4 and Full-Year 2025: A Sharp Reversal

Results reported around January 28, 2026 showed a marked slowdown from Q3's momentum:

- **Q4 revenue**: $24.901 billion, down 3% YoY, as automotive revenue fell 11% YoY to $17.693 billion. Energy generation/storage revenue remained a bright spot, growing 25% YoY to $3.837 billion.
- **Q4 deliveries**: 418,227 vehicles, down 16% YoY—a steep reversal from Q3's record.
- **Q4 profitability**: GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders of $840 million (diluted EPS of $0.24, down 60% YoY); non-GAAP net income of $1.761 billion (EPS $0.50).

For **full-year 2025**:
- Total revenue: $94.827 billion, down 3% from $97.690 billion in 2024.
- GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders: $3.794 billion, down 46% YoY.
- Diluted GAAP EPS: $1.08, down 47% YoY; non-GAAP diluted EPS: $1.66, down 28% YoY.
- Automotive gross margin: 17.8% GAAP (down from 18.4% in 2024); flat at 15.4% excluding regulatory credits.
- Free cash flow: $6.220 billion, up 74% YoY, with cash and investments growing 21% YoY to $44.059 billion.

The energy business continued to outperform: full-year energy revenue rose 27% YoY to $12.771 billion, and energy gross profit hit a record $1.1 billion in Q4—the segment's fifth consecutive record quarter.

## Strategic Developments

**Musk's $1 Trillion Pay Package**: On November 6, 2025, Tesla shareholders approved a landmark compensation plan for Elon Musk potentially worth **$1 trillion in stock**, passing with over 75% of votes cast. The package is contingent on ambitious milestones, including delivering 20 million vehicles and 1 million robots, alongside stock and market-cap targets. Shares fell nearly 4% the following day amid broader market weakness.

**Robotaxi and Cybercab Rollout**: Tesla launched its purpose-built Cybercab robotaxi around early September 2025, though the launch reportedly disappointed investors, with one analyst warning of downside risk under certain valuation scenarios. The NHTSA has since begun reviewing the Cybercab rollout. By December 2025, Tesla began testing driverless Robotaxis in Austin, and in January 2026 it started removing safety monitors from customer rides on a limited basis. The Bay Area ride-hailing service expanded to serve San Jose Airport, with plans to extend Robotaxi service in the first half of 2026 to Dallas, Houston, Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas.

**Optimus Robot**: A Gen 3 version—described as the first design intended for mass production—is planned for unveiling in Q1 2026, with production slated to begin before the end of 2026 and an eventual capacity target of 1 million robots per year.

**AI Infrastructure**: Tesla is building "Cortex 2" at Gigafactory Texas and developing in-house AI5 (2027 production) and AI6 (2028) inference chips, targeting a 50x performance improvement over AI4. FSD v14 has rolled out, with the fleet now collecting over 500 years of continuous driving data daily. The company also entered a framework agreement with xAI as part of its "Master Plan Part IV."

**Other Production Plans**: New production lines are planned for the Tesla Semi and Cybercab (starting first half of 2026), Megapack 3/Megablock production at Megafactory Houston (2026), and continued development of the next-generation Roadster.

## Analyst Sentiment

Wall Street remains sharply divided on Tesla. In early December 2025, a new Morgan Stanley analyst downgraded the stock from "Overweight" to "Equal-Weight," citing valuation concerns—even while raising the price target to around $425. Shares fell 3–4% on the news, and Morgan Stanley simultaneously downgraded other EV makers, including Rivian. This followed an earlier round of Morgan Stanley activity in which the firm downgraded Tesla to equal-weight while raising its price target to $250 from $200, suggesting multiple downgrade events occurred across 2025 with shifting targets.

Not all recent moves have been negative: DBS Bank upgraded Tesla to "Hold" on November 5, 2025. Overall, analyst price targets span an enormous range—from a low of **$115** to a high of **$600**—reflecting deep disagreement over how to value Tesla's AI/robotics optionality against its core automotive fundamentals.

## Key Risk Themes

1. **Margin compression amid revenue growth**: Q3 2025 combined record revenue and deliveries with a ~40% YoY drop in operating income, reinforcing concerns that growth is coming at the expense of profitability.
2. **Automotive demand volatility**: Q4's 11% YoY decline in automotive revenue marked a sharp reversal from Q3's delivery record, potentially reflecting reduced U.S. EV tax credits and competitive pressures.
3. **Full-year profit decline**: Despite the strong AI/robotics narrative, FY2025 GAAP EPS fell 47% YoY and net income fell 46% YoY—highlighting a gap between current financial performance and the long-duration bets (robotaxi, Optimus, AI chips) underpinning the stock's valuation.
4. **Extreme valuation and analyst disagreement**: A P/E multiple exceeding 300–400x, combined with a $115–$600 analyst price-target range, illustrates the market's struggle to price Tesla as both an automaker and a speculative AI/robotics company.
5. **Governance and key-person risk**: The $1 trillion Musk pay package ties substantial potential dilution and compensation to speculative long-term milestones, raising governance concerns among some shareholders and analysts.
6. **Regulatory scrutiny**: NHTSA's review of the Cybercab robotaxi rollout represents an ongoing regulatory risk, particularly as Tesla removes safety monitors from Austin robotaxis in early 2026.
